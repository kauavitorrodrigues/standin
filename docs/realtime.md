# Real-time and media guide

How people see each other, talk and chat inside a space. How many people it
holds and what it costs are in [performance.md](performance.md).

## Real-time presence

Teammates in the same space see each other move, emote and talk in real time over Socket.IO. The server relays positions, emotes, nearby messages and typing, and decides who hears what: only people in range, and only people in the same private room.

How a position travels:

1. The browser sends its position when it changes, at most every 80 ms.
2. The server collects the changes and sends each person one batch at a fixed tick, with only the people inside their area of interest.
3. A person who enters or leaves view is announced separately, so the avatar appears and disappears at the right time.

Two more things travel with the position. Dancing is a flag of the position (`isDancing`, a bit next to the sitting one in the position codec), so a person who arrives late sees who is dancing in the same tuple, and the start and the end are sent at once. Confetti is its own event, `space:confetti` (no payload, no ack), which the server rate limits at 3 per second with a burst of 6 and relays only to the people inside the 640 px event radius and in the same private room, stamped with the sender's `socketId`.

**Emotes, confetti and dance.** The keys 1 to 8 send an emote, `F` throws confetti and holding `Z` dances. All of them are off while the chat or another overlay holds the keyboard, and `Z` also ignores Shift, Ctrl, Alt and Meta. Confetti is limited to one burst per 500 ms on the sender, and the 🎉 reaction throws the same burst. A dance is not a toggle: it ends when the key is released, when the person moves, sits or jumps to another spot, when the window loses focus or the tab is hidden, or after 60 seconds (the server cuts it at 62 s and a capped socket cannot start again until it sends a position that is not dancing). A person who is sitting cannot dance, and an emote sent while dancing is dropped. The dance reuses the emote frames of the avatar. With reduced motion, confetti draws nothing and the dance holds the emote pose.

**Footsteps.** Your own steps and those of the people in earshot play a take for the material of the `ground` tile under their feet (`CARPET`, `WOOD`, `STONE` or `GRASS`; an empty cell, a tile with no material or an unknown tileset sounds as wood). There are 17 takes for each material in `apps/web/public/sounds/footsteps/<material>/`, at the same loudness, so only the game sets the volume. A person makes at most one step sound every 214 ms (the first one at once, and a silent step does not use the window), and never the same take twice in a row. The sound is silent from 9 tiles (the proximity preset can shorten that reach, never widen it), and the volume is the proximity curve times 0.8, times the output volume of the person, so 0 mutes the footsteps. Someone the rules cannot hear, such as another private room, is silent. Every take loads when the map scene starts, and a take that fails to load stays silent without stopping the walk. The takes are CC BY 4.0 and are made by the seeded `generate.sh` of that folder (it needs ffmpeg with libmp3lame). The material of a tile is set in the tileset editor, see the [map editor guide](map-editor.md).

The server keeps who is inside each space in memory, so joining reads only the person who joins. Every connection is rate limited per event. The same socket carries the editor lock, the "map published" notices, and the room events (open, lock, join request, crossing a door). The numbers behind this (tick, areas, caches and limits) are in the [performance guide](performance.md).

## Proximity voice, camera and screen sharing

Audio, camera and screen sharing go through a media server (an SFU) that runs inside the API process. Each browser sends every stream once, to the server, and the server forwards it to the people who should receive it. The server never decodes or mixes media. The code is in `packages/media`, the signalling is the `media:*` events of `packages/contracts`, and the browser side is `SfuClient` in `apps/web/src/features/game/multiplayer/lib`.

**The server decides who receives what.** A pure planner (`planSubscriptions`) runs on a tick of 250 ms, only for the people whose surroundings changed. The client never asks for a stream, it plays the consumers the server creates for it. Someone out of range receives no media at all, not just a lowered volume.

**Camera quality.** A camera is sent as three layers (low, mid and high) and the server forwards one of them to each receiver. The browser tells the server which one each tile needs from the real width of the tile on screen (CSS pixels times the pixel ratio): under 240 px is low, under 560 px is mid, and from there it is high. The strip of small tiles is always low. In the full view the featured tile, or every tile of a small grid, is measured and the layer follows the window when it is resized. The browser sends this as `media:set-focus` with a list of `{ userId, layer }` (at most 8), and a person who is not listed arrives in the low layer. A weak machine is always kept on the low layer.

| Capture    | Low                       | Mid                       | High                        |
| ---------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Up to 720p | 320x180, 150 kbps, 15 fps | 640x360, 500 kbps, 24 fps | 1280x720, 2 Mbps, 24 fps    |
| 1080p      | 480x270, 200 kbps, 15 fps | 960x540, 900 kbps, 30 fps | 1920x1080, 3.5 Mbps, 30 fps |

**Camera resolution.** The default is 360p and the person chooses up to 1080p at 30 fps in the camera settings. The choice is capped by the smaller of two limits, so a crowded space or a weak machine does not get a resolution it cannot carry:

- The space: up to 8 people allow 1080p, up to 25 allow 720p, and above that 480p. A limit is kept until the count falls 2 people below it, so someone joining and leaving at the border does not restart the camera.
- The machine: 4 cores or fewer, or 4 GB of memory or less (the memory is reported only by some browsers), keeps it at 720p and hides 1080p from the list. A browser that hides these values counts as capable.

When the capture changes between 720p and 1080p the producer of the camera is made again, because it keeps the encodings it was created with.

**How to read the stage.** The stage lists the people near you, nearest first, with an avatar until their camera is on. A tile gets a video only when the server sends it, which is why only two cameras play at once. The speaking indicators come from the server and show only people you can hear.

**Joining.** After `space:join` is answered the browser asks for the capabilities of the router, opens a send and a receive transport, and produces its microphone, then the camera and the screen when they are on. The microphone state (muted or not) lives in the presence of the space and in the join snapshot, so a person who arrives later sees it. The privacy modes (`media:set-privacy`, announced with `media:privacy-changed`) work the same way, and the browser sends them again whenever a media session begins.

**Soundboard.** A clip is mixed with the microphone in the browser, so it rides the audio producer the person already has and the media server adds no transport. The `soundboard:play` and `soundboard:stop` events (with an ack) only carry state: the server checks that the clip is visible to the organization of the space, lets a person have up to `SOUNDBOARD_MAX_CONCURRENT_CLIPS` clips playing at once (the same clip can overlap itself, each play has its own `playId`) and keeps a short cooldown between starts (`SOUNDBOARD_COOLDOWN_MS`), and tells the space with `soundboard:started` and `soundboard:stopped`. Muting silences the voice before the track, not the clip, so while a clip plays the person counts as unmuted for the planner and the speaker ranking (`soundboardActive`). The `muted` state shown to the others does not change. Guests have no clips.

**Announcing.** Any member, but not a guest, can announce to the people around them, for a talk or a call to the room. It is a megaphone in the control bar of the office view, blocked while the microphone is muted (muting during an announcement ends it). The state lives only in the presence, with no table, and there is no limit of announcers and no timeout. `space:set-announcing` with `{ announcing }` answers `{ ok, announcingSince }`, and the server sends `space:announcers` with the whole list of announcers inside the 640 px event radius and in the same private room, oldest first and without yourself, only when the list changes. While you announce a banner says **Você está anunciando** with a **Parar** button, and while someone near you announces it says who. A sound plays when a new announcer comes into earshot. The errors are `ANNOUNCING_NOT_ALLOWED` (403, a guest) and `ANNOUNCING_INVALID` (422), and the event is limited to 2 per second with a burst of 6.

By default an announcer receives no camera from the people who listen, only their screen shares: the planner drops the camera when the receiver is announcing and the person has not opted in (`withholdsCamera`). A listener opts in with the **Compartilhar vídeo** button of the banner, which sends `space:share-video-with-announcer` with `{ sharing }`. The button is hidden for guests, for the announcer and while the camera is off. Starting needs an announcer in earshot (`SHARE_VIDEO_NO_ANNOUNCER`, 409), guests are refused (`SHARE_VIDEO_NOT_ALLOWED`, 403), and stopping is always allowed. The choice ends by itself when no announcer is left near the person and when the camera is turned off, so turning it on again asks again.

**Raising a hand.** Any member, but not a guest, can raise a hand in the grid view of everybody in the space (on the map people just talk), and the hand goes down when the person leaves the grid. It follows the same shape as announcing: `space:set-hand-raised` with `{ raised }` answers `{ ok, raisedSince }`, and `space:raised-hands` carries the whole list, oldest first, within earshot and the same room. The raised hand is a badge on the camera tile of the person, which waves once (still under reduced motion). People nearby get a toast with the avatar and a two-note chime when a new hand goes up. The errors are `HAND_RAISE_NOT_ALLOWED` (403) and `HAND_RAISE_INVALID` (422), with the same limit of 2 per second and a burst of 6. Both states are in memory only.

**Status.** A person is active, away or busy, and may add a custom status (an optional emoji and up to 80 characters, with an optional end up to 24 hours ahead). Busy is the headphones mode and keeps its rule for audio and video; it is not sent as a status, the others read it from `media:privacy-changed`. Away is a real mode: the person is out of the office. The avatar of an away person stays where it was, standing still, and the others keep seeing it, with a moon in place of the dot on its name tag and "away" in the profile card. The server keeps the person in the presence of the space with the availability and the custom status, and the media rules treat away like headphones (`awayRule`): nobody hears or sees the person and the person hears and sees nobody. The seat of someone who was sitting stays held, since the avatar is still there. Rooms, doors and the desk of the person are not affected. The browser shows a bluish layer with a light blur over the game, with no input to the game, hides the controls of the bar and shows the avatar, the name, "Ausente" and a button to return to the office. Both kinds of away enter this mode: the one chosen by hand and the one derived after 5 minutes without mouse, keyboard, touch, wheel or focus on the window and without speech into the person's own microphone (`IDLE_AWAY_AFTER_MS`; a muted or off microphone adds nothing). Neither ends by itself on activity: the person returns with the button, or by choosing active or busy in the card of the own avatar (busy returns with the headphones on). `space:set-status` with `{ availability, customStatus }` (`availability` is `active` or `away`, `customStatus` is the status or `null`) answers `{ ok, status }`, and the server sends `space:status` to the rest of the space; `space:joined` and `space:peer-joined` carry both fields. Nothing is stored in the database: the server keeps the status in the presence, clears a custom status by itself when its end is reached (a timer per connection, cancelled when the person leaves) and tells the space, and the browser keeps the choices on the device and sends the status again on every join. The errors are `STATUS_NOT_ALLOWED` (403, outside a space) and `STATUS_INVALID` (422), with the limit of 2 per second and a burst of 6.

**When the planner runs.** The planner looks again at a person who moved (a new x or y, a repeated position is ignored), started or stopped sending, muted, changed a privacy mode, opened their receive transport, left, or whose private room changed. It looks at everybody near them too, because their surroundings changed.

**Failures.** If a transport fails, or stays disconnected past the grace period, the client first tries an ICE restart of that transport: it asks the server for new ICE parameters (`media:restart-ice`) and applies them, up to 4 times with an exponential backoff, without touching the device, the other transport or the producers. Only when the restarts run out, or the server refuses, does it ask the server to drop its session (`media:restart`), wait with an exponential backoff and builds a new one, producing again what it was sending. If the media worker dies the API starts a new one and tells every client to rebuild (`media:reset`). Chat and movement are not affected. A machine under CPU pressure asks the server for the low layer and fewer streams.

**Volume** is a local curve: full strength up to about 5 tiles, then an exponential falloff to a floor of 0.1. The client plays at most 8 remote audio streams (4 on a weak machine).

How far this goes, and the capacity model, is in [docs/performance.md](performance.md). The camera starts off and is opened only when you turn it on.

## The control bar

The bar floats over the office and is the footer of the chat panel. The buttons that depend on the situation (soundboard, announce, raise hand, nearby chat and screen share) glide in and out through `PresenceSlot` (`apps/web/src/components/ui/presence-slot.tsx`): the width opens so the neighbours move aside, while the button scales and fades in, with a soft spring. It is a generic wrapper for any item of a flex row (`show`, and `halfGap` for the gap of the parent), and it is instant under reduced motion. In the chat footer, where the column is narrow, the microphone and the camera are compact: they lose the device menu and its divider, which animate out the same way, so every button fits. The device menu is available in the office bar.

## Chat (spaces and DMs)

Every space has one standing `SPACE` conversation shared by all of that space's members, for talking without being in proximity range. On top of that, any two members of the same organization can open a `DIRECT` conversation with each other. Both support text and file-attachment messages, editing, reactions, threads and read receipts, and both are scoped to the organization.

**The composer** is a rich text field (TipTap) that holds one paragraph of plain text: what you type is what is stored. Enter sends, Shift+Enter breaks the line, and Enter only confirms the candidate while an IME composition is open. A message holds up to 4000 characters, and a paste is cut at the limit. Editing a message uses the same field.

**Mentions.** Typing `@` in the conversation of a space opens the list of the people of the organization (not yourself), online first, each group by name, filtered by what follows the `@`. Picking one inserts a chip, saved in the content as `[@Name](mention:<userId>)`. A direct conversation has no mentions: the `@` does nothing there. The server never trusts the ids of the client: it reads them from the content, before anything is uploaded, on create and on edit, and answers 400 when:

- a message has more than 20 mentions (`MENTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`),
- a direct conversation has any (`MENTION_IN_DIRECT_CONVERSATION`),
- a mentioned person is not an active member of the organization (`MENTION_INVALID_USER`),
- the author mentions themselves (`MENTION_SELF`).

The table `message_mentions` is what the server trusts, and it lets an edit tell who was added or removed. The API returns `mentions: [{ userId, name }]` with the current name, so a renamed person shows the new name in old messages, and a removed account shows "Usuário removido". `chat:message-created` and `chat:message-updated` carry `mentions` and a `mentionsMe` flag for each recipient. The mentioned person gets a notification titled "<name> mencionou você" with sound, instead of the normal one, even when the conversation is muted. Only the master notifications switch turns it off. An edit notifies only the people it adds, so keeping a mention does not notify again.

## Running the media server on a laptop

`pnpm install` downloads a prebuilt worker. If none exists for the platform it builds one, which needs Python 3 and a C++ compiler. Set `MEDIA_ANNOUNCED_ADDRESS=127.0.0.1` in `.env` to test on one machine, or the LAN address of the laptop to test with another computer. In Docker, publish the media port over UDP and TCP (the `app` profile of `compose.yml` does it).
